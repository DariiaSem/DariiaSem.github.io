Task 02

The Zero Sum Mindset

Game theory dictates how corporations interact. Discuss these fundamental approaches to negotiation with your instructor.

The Zero Sum Game

A situation where one participant can only gain exactly what the other participant loses. There is no mutual benefit.

Discussion: Are corporate mergers truly zero sum or is it possible to construct a scenario where both entities genuinely win?

The Prisoners Dilemma

A paradox where two individuals acting in their own self interest do not produce the optimal outcome for either party.

Discussion: How do you convince a rival firm to cooperate when betraying you offers them an immediate financial reward?

Strategic Idioms

Corporate negotiation relies heavily on multi word idioms. The preposition completely controls the direction of the action.

Strategic IdiomMeaningExample in context
Cave in toTo surrender to demands after initially resisting.They eventually caved in to the pressure of the shareholders.
Hold out forTo wait and refuse to agree until you get exactly what you want.Do not accept the first offer. Hold out for a higher valuation.
Double down onTo significantly increase your risk or commitment to a strategy.Instead of retreating they decided to double down on their bluff.
Back out ofTo withdraw from an agreement or commitment.They tried to back out of the contract at the last minute.
Play onTo exploit a weakness or an emotion to gain an advantage.The negotiator tried to play on their fear of bankruptcy.

Task 09

Narrative Alignment

To recount a negotiation flawlessly you must differentiate the chronological sequence of events using past tenses.

The Main Timeline

  • Past Simple: Used for the core sequence of events. A happened then B happened then C happened.

    We entered the room, delivered the ultimatum, and walked out.

The Flashback

  • Past Perfect: Used to clarify that an action was completely finished before the main timeline began.

    Before we entered the room, we had already secured the proxy votes.
