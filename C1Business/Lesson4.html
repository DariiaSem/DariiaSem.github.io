Task 3 · Vocabulary

The Nomad Lexicon

Hover over each card to reveal the definition. These are your eight target words for deconstructing tourism.

Transient
Lasting only for a short time; remaining in place only briefly.
Commodify
To turn something like culture into a product that can be bought and sold.
Homogenize
To make things uniform or similar, destroying local uniqueness.
Gentrification
The process of changing a neighborhood, making it too expensive for original residents.
Ephemeral
Fading quickly; existing only briefly.
Curated
Carefully chosen and organized to look perfect, often hiding reality.
Saturation
The state when a place is too full of tourists to accept any more.
Disillusionment
A feeling of disappointment when discovering something is not as good as believed.

Task 4 · Reading

The Bali Mirage

Read the short story below. Notice the bolded verbs. We will use them to analyze narrative tenses next.

The sun was setting over the rice terraces when Mark finally realized the truth. He had been living in Bali for six months, chasing the perfect digital nomad dream he had seen online.


Before he moved here, he had imagined a life of deep spiritual connection and quiet focus. Instead, while he was typing emails at a trendy cafe, he noticed that every person around him was doing exactly the same thing. They were participating in a heavily curated illusion.


The local culture had completely commodified itself to serve the transient population. Disillusionment washed over him. He packed his laptop, paid for his overpriced matcha, and walked back to his villa. He had been searching for authenticity, but he had only contributed to the homogenization of the island.

  • How long had he been living there before the realization hit him?

  • Did he find the authenticity he had been looking for?

Task 7 · Grammar Theory

Narrative Tenses Alignment

To tell a compelling story, C1 speakers weave four distinct past tenses together to create depth and timeline clarity.

TenseFunction in NarrativeExample from text
Past SimpleThe main events. Moves the story forward in sequence.He packed his laptop and walked away.
Past ContinuousThe background atmosphere. Setting the scene.The sun was setting over the terraces.
Past PerfectThe flashback. A finished action before the main story timeline.He had imagined a spiritual life.
Past Perfect Cont.The extended flashback. Emphasizes the duration of a prior action.He had been living there for six months.
⚠️ Watch out: Do not overuse the Past Perfect. Once you establish the flashback timeline, you can return to the Past Simple.
