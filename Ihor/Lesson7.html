Task 3 · Vocabulary

The Existentialist's Lexicon

Hover over each card to reveal the definition. Twelve target concepts for today.

Absurdity
The clash between our hunger for meaning and a universe that offers none.
Authenticity
Living in line with your true values rather than social expectations.
Bad faith
Sartre's term for deceiving yourself to avoid freedom and responsibility.
Angst / dread
A deep, objectless anxiety about freedom, choice and death.
Nihilism
The belief that life has no inherent meaning, value or purpose.
Alienation
Feeling estranged from other people, work, or even yourself.
Mortality
The fact that we will die; awareness of our own finiteness.
Introspection
Examining your own thoughts, motives and feelings.
Persona
The public face or role someone presents to the world.
Redemption
Being saved from guilt or failure; making up for past mistakes.
Ephemeral
Lasting only a very short time.
Self-reckoning
Facing uncomfortable truths about yourself and accepting accountability.

Task 4 · Reading

The Last Screening

Read the story. The highlighted verb forms show how time layers are aligned. Don't analyze yet — just follow the timeline.

The old cinema was closing that night, and Mara sat alone in the third row. She had been coming here every Friday since she moved to the city, and she used to tell herself that the films would eventually explain her life to her.

When the projector flickered to life, she realized she had never watched this particular film all the way through. Years earlier, she had walked out halfway, convinced it was pretentious. Now she suspected she must have been running from something else entirely.

On screen, a man stands on a bridge and asks a stranger why anyone bothers to continue. The stranger doesn't answer; he just hands him a coat. Mara was about to look away when she noticed she was crying. No sooner had the credits started to roll than the lights came on, and the usher told her they would be demolishing the building in the morning.

She walked out into the rain feeling oddly light. She had expected grief; instead, she felt relieved. Had she known that meaning was something she could build rather than find, she would have stopped searching years ago — but she wasn't sure she would have listened. By the time she reached the station, she had decided to call her sister, whom she hadn't spoken to in a decade.

💬 Comprehension

  • What had Mara been hoping to get from films for years?
  • Why had she left the film years earlier? What does she now suspect?
  • Why does the writer switch to the present tense for the film scene?
  • What exactly changed in her attitude by the end?
  • Which existential ideas appear in the story?
Task 5 · Comprehension Check

True or False?

Click the button you think is correct.

1. Mara had seen the whole film before the night of the story.
False — she "had walked out halfway" (Past Perfect: earlier than the main story).
2. She was already crying before the credits began.
True — "she noticed she was crying" happened before "No sooner had the credits started to roll…".
3. The usher said the building was going to be demolished the next morning.
True — "would be demolishing" is future-in-the-past.
4. Mara actually stopped searching for meaning years ago.
False — "Had she known… she would have stopped" is an unreal past: she did NOT stop.
5. She called her sister before she reached the station.
False — "by the time she reached the station, she had decided to call" — only the decision came first.
Task 6 · Grammar Discovery

Match the Tense to Its Job

Go back to the story. For each function, find an example and name the form.

  • An action earlier than the main storyline: had walked out — Past Perfect
  • A long state or activity before a past moment: had been coming — Past Perfect Continuous
  • A past future seen from the past: would be demolishing
  • A past habit or repeated hope: used to / would
  • A logical guess about the past: must have been running
  • Two events in a very tight sequence (formal): No sooner had… than…
  • An imaginary different past: Had she known… would have stopped
🧩 Your hypothesis: Why does the writer use the present tense for the film scene but the past for Mara's own life? What effect does that create?
Task 7 · Grammar Theory

Narrative Tense Alignment: Layers of Time

Think of a story as layers: the main line (what happens), the background (what was going on), the pre-history (what had happened before), and the "future" as seen from inside the story.

PRE-HISTORY

had been coming
BACKGROUND
was closing
was crying
MAIN LINE

FUTURE-IN-THE-PAST
would be demolishing
was about to
FormJobExample
Past SimpleMain storyline, sequence of completed events.She sat down, the lights dimmed, the film began.
Past ContinuousBackground, or an action interrupted by a main-line event.It was raining when she left the cinema.
Past Perfect"Past in the past": earlier than the main line; reason or cause.She realized she had seen it before.
Past Perfect ContinuousDuration or visible cause before a past moment.Her eyes were red; she had been crying for an hour.
Future in the pastLooking forward from a past viewpoint: would, was going to, was about to, was to.She thought the film would change her. / He was to die in the next scene.
Used to / wouldPast habits (would only for actions, not states).She would sit in row three. She used to be certain about everything.
Narrative presentSynopses, jokes, anecdotes, film plots; vivid immediacy.In the final scene, the hero walks away and doesn't look back.
Past modalsDeduction and regret about the past.He must have known. She shouldn't have left. They needn't have worried.
InversionFormal, dramatic sequence.Hardly had it begun when… / Not until it ended did she understand.
Unreal pastRegret and alternate histories.If only I'd asked. / Had I known, I'd have stayed. / I wish he had told me.
⚠️ Alignment rules:
1) Once you choose a time layer, keep it consistent until you deliberately shift.
2) Shifting back one step (backshift) happens when reporting: "I am lost" → she said she was lost; "I was lost" → she said she had been lost.
3) Would as future-in-the-past ≠ would as habit ≠ would in unreal conditionals. Context decides.
4) Past Perfect is only needed when order is unclear. If the sequence is obvious (after, as soon as, because), Past Simple is often natural.
5) You can embed a present-tense film summary inside a past-tense life story, but signal the switch ("On screen…", "In the film…").

Fix the Timeline — Hard Mode

Put the verb in brackets into the correct form. Then check the key. Watch for several layers inside one sentence.

  • By the time the film (end), most of the audience (already / leave), but a few (still / sit) in silence.
  • He (live) in the city for years before he (finally / admit) that he (run) from himself all along.
  • She (not / realize) that her whole routine (be) a mask until a stranger (ask) her who she (be) when nobody (watch).
  • No sooner (the hero / accept) his fate than the camera (pull) back and (show) he (be) alone from the start.
  • The director (say) in an interview that he (already / plan) the ending long before the script (be) finished, but that he (not / know) it (be) going to feel so bleak.
  • Had he (not / meet) her that night, he probably (never / forgive) himself — and yet he (always / wonder) whether it (be) fate or chance.
  • As a child she (would / sit) by the window for hours; she (not / yet / know) that this habit (shape) her whole way of seeing the world.
  • Not until the final credits (roll) (he / understand) why she (smile) throughout the film.

Task 13 · Role-Play

The Film Festival Interview

A critic interviews a director about an existential film. The director must narrate the backstory with accurate tense alignment.

Critic: Ask how the idea for the film first came about.
↓
Director: Narrate: what you had been going through, what you were doing when the idea hit you, what you decided.
↓
Critic: Challenge the bleak ending: "Surely you could have ended it differently?"
↓
Director: Use past modals and an unreal past: "If I had… I would have…"; also future-in-the-past: "I knew the audience would…".
↓
Critic: Ask what the director hoped viewers would take away.
↓
Director: Summarize the plot in the narrative present and finish with a personal reflection.
