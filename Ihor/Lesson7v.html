Theory 1

Past Habits & Vivid Storytelling

When we tell stories about the past, we don't just use the Past Simple. We use specific structures to highlight habits, states, or to bring the listener directly into the action[cite: 4].

Structure Usage & Constraints Example[cite: 4]
Used to Past habits AND past states. Can be used for things that were true but aren't anymore (live, be, know, have). She used to be certain about everything. (State)
Would Past habits/actions ONLY. Cannot be used with stative verbs. Usually requires an established past timeframe. She would sit in row three. (Repeated action)
Narrative Present Using Present Simple/Continuous to tell a past story. Creates vivid immediacy for synopses, jokes, anecdotes, or film plots. In the final scene, the hero walks away and doesn't look back.
⚠️ Watch out: You cannot say "I would live in London." (Live is a state). You must say "I used to live in London."
Theory 2

Deduction, Criticism & Alternate Histories

How do we talk about things we are sure happened, things that were a mistake, or things that never happened at all?[cite: 4]

Category Modals / Structures Example[cite: 4]
Past Modals (Deduction) Must have + V3: 99% sure it happened.
Can't / Couldn't have + V3: 99% sure it didn't. 		He must have known.
Past Modals (Regret/Criticism) Should have / Ought to have + V3: It was a good idea, but didn't happen.
Needn't have + V3: You did it, but it wasn't necessary. 		She shouldn't have left.
They needn't have worried.
Unreal Past (Alternate Histories) If only / I wish + Past Perfect: Regret about the past.
Had I known (Inverted 3rd Conditional): Formal alternate history. 		If only I'd asked.
Had I known, I'd have stayed.
I wish he had told me.
Theory 3

Destiny & Dramatic Tension

Advanced storytelling requires pacing. We use these structures to build anticipation or show what a character believed was going to happen[cite: 4].

Concept Forms Example[cite: 4]
Future in the past Looking forward from a past viewpoint: would, was going to, was about to, was to. (Note: was to implies a destined or formal plan). She thought the film would change her.
He was to die in the next scene.
Inversion Formal, dramatic sequence. Moving a negative adverbial to the front, followed by Auxiliary + Subject. Hardly had it begun when...
Not until it ended did she understand.
