Opp

(Cultural Ref.) A famous haunted house; used metaphorically for something terrifying.

Task 5 · Deep Dive (Ex 2)

Character Motivation: True or False?

Based on the psychological profile painted in Verse 1[cite: 3], decide if these statements about the "protagonist" are True or False.

1. He trusts the people around him to protect his morality.
False — "morality's dust, I lack in trust."
2. The phrase "tripping and falling" represents his physical clumsiness.
False — It represents emotional stumbling, making mistakes, and losing grip on his ego.
3. He believes that adopting a "mask" (a fake persona) solves internal conflict.
False — "But a mask won't hide who you are inside / Look around, the reality's carved in lies."
4. He views his past mistakes as permanent, unforgivable failures.
False — He concludes: "learn to love myself, forgive myself a hundred times, dog."
5. "Done every magazine, what's fame to me?" suggests he finds media attention meaningless.
True — He has achieved the peak of fame and realized it doesn't cure his existential dread.

Task 7 · Grammar Theory

Past Perfect vs. Past Simple

The Past Perfect (had + V3) is the "past of the past." It is crucial for analyzing character motivations[cite: 3], as it explains *why* a character acts a certain way based on their hidden backstory.

Backstory / Flashback
Past Perfect (had + V3)
Main Story Event
Past Simple (V2)
Now
TenseFormCinematic Function
Past SimpleVerb + -ed / V2The main actions moving the plot forward. "The protagonist woke up and looked in the mirror."
Past Perfecthad + Past Participle (V3)The character's backstory or cause of the action. "He realized society had layered him up in lies."
⚠️ Watch out: We only use Past Perfect when we need to show a clear sequence between two past events. If events happen in normal chronological order (Event 1, then Event 2, then Event 3), just use Past Simple for all of them!
Task 8 · Grammar Practice

Fill the Gaps: The Anti-Hero's Origin

Complete each sentence by putting the verbs in brackets into either the Past Simple or the Past Perfect.

  • By the time the movie (end), the audience (already/realize) the hero was actually the villain.
  • He (shut down) emotionally because his friends (betray) him years earlier.
  • The character (look) in the mirror and (see) a monster. (Chronological order)
  • She (never/experience) existential dread until she (lose) her job.
  • When Kendrick (sing) about "Amityville", he (already/face) his darkest fears.
  • The society in the film (collapse) because the leaders (outdo) each other in greed for decades.

  • "She took off her mask. She realized it was all a lie." → Combine using after:
