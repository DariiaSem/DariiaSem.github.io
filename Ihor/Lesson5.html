Task 3 · Vocabulary

The Strategist's Lexicon

Hover over each card to reveal the definition. These are your eight target concepts for today.

Scope Creep
The continuous, uncontrolled growth in a project's requirements after it begins.
Trade-off
A compromise where you lose one quality or feature to gain another.
Buy-in
Agreement, support, or financial commitment from stakeholders for a proposal.
Zero-sum Game
A situation where one person's or feature's gain is exactly equal to another's loss.
Leverage
An advantage that gives you power to influence a situation or negotiation.
Bottleneck
A point of congestion in a system that slows down the overall process.
Velocity
A metric used to measure the amount of work a team can tackle during a single sprint.
Pushback
A negative or resistant reaction to a proposed plan or change.

Task 4 · Reading

The Poker Game of Sprint Planning

Read the scenario below. Notice the highlighted prepositional idioms — we'll deconstruct how they are used strategically.

Sprint planning was always a polite war of attrition. Sarah, the Product Manager, leaned forward. "I know we're tight on capacity, but the client really needs this payment gateway update. Can we just squeeze it into the current sprint?"


Alex, the lead developer, didn't blink. He knew the team's velocity was already stretched. "Sarah, we can definitely do that. But sprint capacity is a zero-sum game. If we factor in the payment gateway, what are we taking off the table?"


Sarah sighed. "Everything currently in the sprint is critical. There's a lot at stake with this release. Can't we just absorb the extra story points?"


"If we try to absorb it, we risk introducing bugs into the core system," Alex countered, calmly pushing back on the request. "That kind of scope creep will ruin our momentum. The analytics dashboard update is currently in the loop for Thursday. If we opt out of that, we can fit your payment gateway. Otherwise, the gateway is out of scope for this week."


By framing the trade-off as a direct choice rather than a flat denial, Alex maintained his leverage. Sarah looked at the board, realized she couldn't argue with the math, and finally caved in. "Fine. Push the dashboard to the backlog. Let's prioritize the gateway."

💬 Comprehension

  • What is Sarah trying to do at the beginning of the meeting?
  • How does Alex respond instead of just saying "no"?
  • Why does Sarah say "there's a lot at stake"?
  • What specific trade-off does Alex offer to make it work?
  • Why was Alex's strategy successful?

Task 7 · Grammar Theory

Prepositions in Strategic Idioms

In tech and business negotiations, prepositional idioms are used to set boundaries, manage expectations, and define what is and isn't possible.

The Proposal
(Put on the table)
The Negotiation (Push back on, Factor in)
The Decision
(Take off table / Cave in)
IdiomLiteral / Physical IdeaStrategic Meaning in Business
On the table / Off the tableSitting on the desk where everyone can see it.Available for discussion as an option (or removed from consideration).
Push back on (something)Physically shoving a heavy object away from you.To resist or disagree with a request or demand, usually with logic.
Factor in / Factor intoPutting an ingredient into a recipe.To include a variable or risk when making a calculation or estimate.
At stakeThe money placed on the table in a betting game.At risk of being lost if a project or negotiation fails.
Out of scopeStanding outside the fence of the property line.Beyond the boundaries of the currently agreed-upon work.
⚠️ Watch out: Don't confuse "push back" (noun) with "push back on" (verb). "We received a lot of pushback from the client." vs. "We need to push back on the client's timeline."

Task 13 · Role-Play

The Mid-Sprint Crisis

Act out this branching conversation between a Lead Developer and a Product Manager regarding a "critical" last-minute request.

PM: Demand a new feature be added immediately, claiming there's a lot "at stake".
↓
Dev: Push back on the timeline. Explain that the team's velocity is full.
↓
PM: Refuse to drop anything. Ask if they can just "work a little faster."
↓
Dev: Use "zero-sum game" or "trade-off". Force the PM to take something "off the table".
↓
PM: Cave in. Reluctantly agree to push a lower-priority item out of scope.
↓
Dev: Confirm the new plan and ensure everyone is "in the loop".
