Task 3 · Vocabulary

The Authenticity Toolkit

Hover over each card to reveal the definition. These are your eight target words for today.

Authenticity
The quality of being genuine, real, or true to one's own character.
Commodification
Turning something — a culture, a tradition — into a product to be sold.
Performative
Done for show or appearance rather than out of genuine feeling.
Curated
Carefully selected and arranged, often to create a particular impression.
Rootlessness
The state of having no stable home, origin, or sense of belonging.
Nostalgia
A sentimental longing for a time or place, often idealized.
Displacement
The state of being moved or forced away from one's usual place.
Belonging
The feeling of being accepted as part of a group, place, or community.

Task 4 · Reading

The Postcard That Lied

Read the story below. Notice the highlighted narrative tenses — we'll come back to them.

Maya had been dreaming about the trip for almost a year when she finally landed in Lombok. She had built an entire online identity around "slow travel" — authenticity, real connections, no tourist traps — and her feed was, frankly, beautifully curated.


So when her guide drove her to a floating village famous for its "untouched" charm, she was already imagining the photo. She was standing on the edge of a wooden pier, phone raised, when she noticed something strange: a staff member was quietly repositioning a fishing net that had drifted out of frame. The "fisherman" smiling beside his boat had, it turned out, finished his actual shift an hour earlier and was now simply posing for tourists who had been arriving all morning. The whole village, she realized, had quietly become a form of commodification — a tradition repackaged as a daily performance for strangers.


Maya had read about this before — a travel writer had once called it "performative authenticity" — but she hadn't expected to witness it herself, mid-shot, phone in hand. While she was deleting the photo, a local teenager who had been watching the whole scene laughed and said, "You're paying for the version of us that looks like what you already believed."


Later that night, Maya scrolled through old photos from her last five countries and felt a strange wave of nostalgia for places that had never really been hers. Maybe that was the real cost of rootlessness and constant displacement — you start collecting borrowed memories of belonging, instead of building your own.

💬 Comprehension

  • What identity had Maya built before this trip?
  • What was she doing right before she noticed something strange?
  • What had actually happened to the "fisherman" before Maya arrived?
  • What did the local teenager say, and what does it mean?
  • Why didn't Maya post the photo?

Task 7 · Grammar Theory

Narrative Tenses Alignment

Good storytelling in English layers four tenses to show what happened first, what was already in progress, and what finally broke through as "the moment."

Earlier past
(had / had been + -ing)
was/were + -ing (background, in progress)
THE MOMENT
(Past Simple)
FormStructureJob in the story
Past Simpleverb + -ed / V2The main, complete events — what actually happened, often the "interrupting" action.
Past Continuouswas/were + V-ingThe background scene — what was already in progress when the main event happened.
Past Perfecthad + V3An earlier, completed event that explains or sets up the main story.
Past Perfect Continuoushad been + V-ingA duration that led up to an earlier point — emphasizing "how long" before the story began.
⚠️ Watch out: "She was packing when the movers arrived" (packing was already happening) vs. "She had packed before the movers arrived" (packing was already finished) — the choice completely changes the picture in the reader's head.
