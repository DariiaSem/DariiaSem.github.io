Task 2 · Reading

Two Rooms

Read Dr. Olena's story about one ordinary shift. Don't worry about grammar labels yet — just enjoy the story and notice how she talks about her experience.

🏥 Two Rooms — by Dr. Olena

7:45 AM. I walk into Operating Room 3. The nurses have already prepared the equipment, and my resident has checked the machine twice — he's still nervous, even after six months. "Have you ever intubated a patient with a difficult airway?" he asks me quietly. I smile. "Many times," I say. "But every airway is different — never assume it will be easy."


At 9:15, the surgery starts. I have never gotten used to how quiet the room becomes at that moment — even after ten years. The surgeon works, the monitors beep steadily, and I watch every number like it's the only thing in the world.


By 1:00 PM, the operation is finished. I walked the patient down to the ICU myself — something I don't always do, but this case was complicated, and I wanted to hand him over personally.


The ICU feels completely different. It's quieter, but somehow more intense. Nurse Kateryna is already waiting. "How did the surgery go?" she asks. "Better than we expected," I tell her, and I give her the full report.


I have worked in this hospital for eight years, and I have seen hundreds of patients move between these two rooms. But I have never stopped feeling that small moment of relief when a patient finally opens their eyes in the ICU, safe.

Quick Check: True or False?

  • The resident has been working with Dr. Olena for six months.
  • Dr. Olena always walks patients to the ICU herself.
  • Dr. Olena has worked at this hospital for eight years.
  • The surgery went worse than expected.
  • Dr. Olena says she never feels relief anymore after ten years of experience.

Task 4 · Grammar Theory

Present Perfect for Experience vs. Past Simple

Now let's check your rule against the real one — and see it on a timeline.

Yesterday, 2 PM
NOW
"I have worked here for 8 years" — the whole cloudy period up to now, no exact point
FormStructureExample
Present Perfecthave/has + past participle (V3)She has managed difficult airways before.
Past SimpleV2 (regular -ed / irregular)She managed a difficult airway yesterday.
Signal Words → Present PerfectSignal Words → Past Simple
ever, never, already, before, so far, in my life, for + period, since + pointyesterday, last week, in 2019, when I was a student, two hours ago, at 2 PM
⚠️ Watch out: "Have you ever...?" asks about your whole life up to now. "Did you...?" asks about one specific, finished moment. Compare: "Have you ever worked a night shift?" vs. "Did you work a night shift last Tuesday?"

Task 6 · Vocabulary Lab

The Language of the OR & ICU

Hover over each card to flip it and see the definition. Try covering the definitions and testing yourself first!

🫁ventilator
a machine that helps a patient breathe when they can't breathe on their own
🧵extubate
to remove the breathing tube from a patient's throat
📟monitor
the screen that shows heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen levels
😴sedation
a calm, sleep-like state produced by medication
❤️vital signs
key measurements like pulse, blood pressure, temperature, and breathing rate
🩸central line
a tube placed into a large vein, often in the neck or chest, for medication or fluids
🔄handover
passing information about a patient from one team to the next
🚨alarm
a sound the monitor makes when something needs attention immediately

Task 12 · Role-Play

OR → ICU Handover

Nurse: Ask how the surgery went.
↓
Doctor: Give a short summary. Mention one thing that happened during surgery (Past Simple).
↓
Nurse: Ask whether the patient has woken up yet.
↓
Doctor: Answer, and mention any medication you have already given (Present Perfect).
↓
Nurse: Confirm you will monitor vital signs, and thank the doctor.
