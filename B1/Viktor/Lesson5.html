Task 3 · Vocabulary

The Cause & Effect Lexicon

Hover over each card to reveal the definition. These verbs and nouns are essential for describing medical outcomes.

Trigger (v.)
To cause a strong reaction or event to happen immediately.
Monitor (v.)
To watch and check a situation carefully over a period of time.
React (v.)
To act in a particular way as a direct result of something else.
Delay (n./v.)
A period of time when you must wait for something; to make something late.
Administer (v.)
To give medicine or medical help to a patient.
Contingency (n.)
A backup plan for an event that might happen in the future.
Symptom (n.)
A physical or mental sign that shows you have an illness.
Prescribe (v.)
To officially tell someone to use a specific medicine or treatment.
Task 4 · Reading

The Night Shift Contingency

Read the scenario below. Notice the sentences starting with "If" — we'll deconstruct how they are used.

Dr. Semenova reviewed the charts for the night shift. In surgery, rules are simple and scientific: if a patient's blood pressure drops rapidly, the machine sounds an alarm. You don't guess; you react. If you administer the wrong dose of anesthesia, the patient experiences severe complications. These are the medical facts she relies on every day.


However, tonight was unpredictable. The weather outside was terrible, and a storm was coming. She turned to the head nurse and discussed their contingency plan.


"Listen," Dr. Semenova said. "If the power goes out tonight, the backup generators will turn on automatically. But we need to be ready. If the generators fail, we won't be able to monitor the vitals electronically. We will have to do it manually."


The nurse nodded. "What about the emergency transport?"


"If the roads flood, the ambulance will delay the transfer of the trauma patient. Make sure we have enough supplies prepared."

Task 7 · Grammar Theory

The Zero Conditional

Used for facts, routines, and general truths. If condition A happens, result B *always* happens.

If + Present Simple
(The Condition)
Present Simple
(The Certain Result)

"If you heat ice, it melts."

"If a patient stops breathing, you start CPR."

Task 9 · Grammar Theory

The First Conditional

Used for real future possibilities. It describes a specific situation in the future and its likely result.

If + Present Simple
(The Possible Future Condition)
Will / Won't + Verb
(The Likely Future Result)

"If it rains tomorrow, I will stay at home."

"If the traffic is bad, we won't arrive on time."

⚠️ Watch out: We NEVER use "will" immediately after "if".
Wrong: "If it will rain..."
Right: "If it rains..."

Task 11 · Pronunciation

Fast Speech: Contractions

In fluent spoken English, we rarely say "will" or "will not". We contract them. Click 🔊 to hear the rhythm.

If I go, I'll call you. (/aɪl/)
If it hurts, we'll stop. (/wiːl/)
If you don't rest, you won't recover. (/wəʊnt/ - do not say "want")

Task 14 · Role-Play

The Anxious Patient

Act out this scenario. Use the First Conditional to explain possibilities and the Zero Conditional to state medical facts.

Teacher: The Patient

You are very nervous about your upcoming surgery. Ask a lot of "What if..." questions. (e.g., "What will happen if I wake up during the surgery? What if I feel sick afterward?")

You: The Doctor

Calm the patient down. Answer their questions clearly.

Linguistic Target: "If you feel sick, we will give you medicine." (First Conditional). "If we monitor your brainwaves, you don't wake up." (Zero Conditional).
