Task 06

The Rules: Simple vs. Continuous

Let's clarify when to use which tense during your hospital rounds.

1. Present Simple (Routines & Facts)

Used for actions that happen regularly, permanent situations, and state verbs (love, know, believe, understand).

"I check the monitors every 5 minutes."

Effect: This is standard protocol. A routine.

2. Present Continuous (Right Now & Temporary)

Used for actions happening exactly at this moment, or temporary situations (like covering a shift this week).

"The patient is waking up."

Effect: Immediate action happening right in front of you.

Task 08

State Verbs & Exceptions

Some verbs in medicine refer to states of mind or senses. They are almost never used with "-ing".

Standard State Verbs

  • › Know (I know the dosage, NOT I am knowing)
  • › Understand (I understand the protocol)
  • › Need (The patient needs oxygen)
  • › Seem (He seems stable)

The "Think" Exception

  • › State (Opinion): I think it's a bad idea. (Don't use -ing)
  • › Action (Process): Shh, I am thinking about the diagnosis. (Use -ing)
