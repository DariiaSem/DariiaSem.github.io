Task 06

A Doctor's Itinerary

Read about the anesthesiologist's recent travel. Notice the difference between Past Simple and Present Perfect.

Have you ever traveled for work? I have visited several European cities for medical summits. In fact, I flew to Geneva last year for the World Health Assembly.

The trip was exhausting. I had a long layover in Warsaw, and when I finally arrived at my hotel, my jet lag was terrible. However, the conference went very well, and I even went sightseeing near Lake Geneva.

Since then, I have booked another flight. I am going to Berlin next month. I haven't received my itinerary yet, but my hospital has already approved the reimbursement.

Syntax Analysis 1

Why does the speaker use "flew to Geneva" (Past) but "I have visited" (Present Perfect)? What is the difference in time?

Syntax Analysis 2

Look at the word "yet" in the last paragraph. Which tense does it almost always pair with?

Task 07

Grammar: Past Simple

The Past Simple is used for actions that are completely finished in a defined time period.

The "Archive" Tense

Use it when the time period is over. You must use specific time markers (yesterday, last week, in 2023, when I was a student).

"I flew to Berlin last year."

Last year is finished. The action is closed.

Task 09

Grammar: Present Perfect

The Present Perfect connects the past to the present moment.

The "Life Experience & Results" Tense

Use it when the time period is not finished (today, this week) or when talking about general life experiences without a specific date (ever, never, already, yet).

"I have visited Geneva."

In my life (unfinished time). The exact date doesn't matter; the experience does.

Task 12

Conference Networking

Practice networking at a medical summit using both tenses organically.

You: The Attendee

You are attending a burn surgery conference. You meet a doctor from Zurich during the coffee break. Introduce yourself.

Linguistic Target: Ask "Have you ever...?" questions (e.g., "Have you ever visited Lviv?"). Then transition to Past Simple to discuss your flight ("My flight was delayed yesterday").

Teacher: The Doctor from Zurich

You have been to many conferences. You love small talk but you hate jet lag.

Linguistic Target: Respond using Past Simple for specific events ("Yes, I visited Ukraine in 2021") and Present Perfect for ongoing states ("I haven't slept yet").
Task 13

Pronunciation Lab

The Past Simple "-ed" ending has three distinct sounds. Master them to sound fluent.

1. The /t/ sound
After voiceless sounds (k, p, s, ch, sh, f).
Worked, Missed, Watched.
2. The /d/ sound
After voiced sounds and vowels (l, n, r, g, v, w, y, z).
Arrived, Played, Traveled.
3. The /id/ sound
Only after 'T' or 'D'. This adds an extra syllable!
Visited, Landed, Attended.
