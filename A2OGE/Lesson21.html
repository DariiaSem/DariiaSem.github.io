Task 2 · Vocabulary Discovery

Communication Tools

🗣️

1. Body Language

The movements or positions of your body that show other people how you are feeling.

Her body language showed that she was very nervous.

👋

2. Gesture

A movement of the hands, arms, or head to express an idea or feeling.

He made a rude gesture at the driver who cut him off.

🤫

3. Whisper

To speak very quietly, using the breath but not the voice.

You have to whisper because the baby is sleeping.

😎

4. Slang

Very informal language that is usually spoken rather than written.

Teenagers use a lot of slang that their parents don't understand.

🧍

5. Posture

The way in which someone usually holds their shoulders, neck, and back.

Sitting at a desk all day is bad for your posture.

👀

6. Eye Contact

The situation in which two people look at each other's eyes at the same time.

Maintaining eye contact is important during a job interview.

Task 5 · Reading

TED Talk: Your Body Language Shapes Who You Are

Hover over the dotted text to see the Ukrainian translations!

We know that our body language affects how others see us. But can it also change how we see ourselves? Social psychologist Amy Cuddy says 'yes'.

Her research shows that changing your posture for two minutes can change your brain's hormones. If you stand in a "power pose" with your arms open, you feel more confident. So before a big interview, don't sit nervously and whisper to yourself. Stand tall, make eye contact, and fake it until you become it.

Task 7 · Grammar Theory 1

Phrasal Verbs (Communication)

Native speakers use phrasal verbs constantly instead of formal words.

Phrasal VerbMeaningExample
Speak up To talk louder "Can you speak up? I can't hear you."
Bring up To mention a topic "Don't bring up politics at dinner."
Get across To communicate an idea clearly "It was hard to get my point across."
Talk over To interrupt someone "Please don't talk over me."
Point out To draw attention to a fact "I'd like to point out a mistake here."
🖊️

Task 9 · Grammar Theory 2

Question Tags

We use question tags at the end of a sentence to ask for confirmation. They are very common in spoken English.

⚖️ The Golden Rule

Positive statement ➔ Negative tag.
Negative statement ➔ Positive tag.

✅ "You are learning English, aren't you?"
✅ "She didn't call, did she?"
⚠️ Tricky Exceptions
I am ➔ aren't I? (I am late, aren't I?)
Let's ➔ shall we? (Let's go, shall we?)
Imperative (orders) ➔ will you? (Close the door, will you?)

Task 12 · Role-Play

The Awkward Interview

You are texting a friend right after a terrible job interview. Complete the chat using the new vocab and phrasal verbs.

Friend
Hey! How did the interview go? You did well, [didn't you / did you]?
You
It was terrible! I had awful [slang / posture] and I couldn't make [eye contact / gestures].
Friend
Oh no. Did you manage to [get across / talk over] your experience at least?
You
Barely. And the manager kept [bringing up / speaking up] my lack of skills. I wanted to cry!
