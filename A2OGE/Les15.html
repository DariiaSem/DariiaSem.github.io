Deduction in Wildlife Tracking

Wildlife biologists often track animals using clues. They use Modals of Deduction to analyze the evidence.

EvidenceModal DeductionMeaning
Fresh warm tracks in the mud The tiger must be close by. 99% Certain (True)
Broken branches high up a tree It might be an elephant or a storm. 50% Possible
No wolves seen in 80 years The species can't be alive in this region. 99% Certain (False / Impossible)

Task 09

Case Study: The Return of the Wolf

In 1995, gray wolves were reintroduced to Yellowstone National Park after being absent for nearly 70 years. Scientists wanted to see what would happen if a top predator returned.

The results were astonishing. Without wolves, deer had overpopulated the park and eaten all the young trees. When wolves returned, deer changed their behavior and avoided open valleys. Consequently, forests regenerated, birds returned, and beaver populations multiplied by ten!

This proves that if you restore one key species, the entire natural habitat heals itself.

Task 10

Second Conditional (Hypothetical & Unreal)

We use the Second Conditional to imagine unreal, impossible, or unlikely situations in the present or future.

Formula & Structure

If + Past Simple , would + Base Verb (V1)

✅ "If I had a billion dollars, I would buy a rainforest to protect it." (Unreal present: I don't have a billion dollars).

✅ "If humans stopped poaching, tigers would not become extinct." (Hypothetical future).

💡 Pro-Tip: "Were" instead of "Was": In formal English and conditionals, we use were for all subjects (I, he, she, it).
"If I were the president, I would ban deforestation."
