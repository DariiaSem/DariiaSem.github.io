Traveling the world, you quickly learn that 'normal' is relative. What is polite in one country can be extremely rude in another.

Task 08

Reported Speech (Statements)

When we tell someone what another person said in the past, we use Reported Speech. To do this, we usually shift the tense exactly "one step back" into the past.

The Tense Shift (Present → Past)

Direct Speech ("Quotes")Reported Speech (No Quotes)
"I love learning about cultures," she said. (Present Simple) She said (that) she loved learning about cultures. (Past Simple)
"We are traveling to Asia," he told me. (Present Continuous) He told me (that) they were traveling to Asia. (Past Continuous)
"I will visit Spain," John said. (Future) John said (that) he would visit Spain. (Conditional)
"I can't speak Japanese," she said. (Modal) She said (that) she couldn't speak Japanese. (Modal Shift)
💡 Note on "Say" vs. "Tell": We use tell when we mention the listener (He told me...). We use say when we don't (He said...). The word "that" is optional.

Task 10

East vs. West: Communication

Many researchers have studied the difference between Eastern and Western cultures. Western cultures tend to be very individualistic.

Communication in the West is usually very direct. However, in the East, group harmony (collectivism) is often more important. Communication is more indirect so that nobody feels offended.

Task 11

Past Perfect in Reported Speech

What happens if the original sentence was ALREADY in the past? We must shift it to the Past Perfect (had + V3).

Direct Speech ("Quotes")Reported Speech (No Quotes)
"I visited Japan," he said. (Past Simple) He said (that) he had visited Japan. (Past Perfect)
"We have seen the festival," they told me. (Present Perfect) They told me (that) they had seen the festival. (Past Perfect)

Note: Both Past Simple and Present Perfect shift back to the Past Perfect!
