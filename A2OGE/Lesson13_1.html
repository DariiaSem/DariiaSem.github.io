Task 3: Word 1

The Action — try on

Meaning
  • Are you buying the clothes when you do this? (Not yet).
  • Are you putting them on to see if the size is correct? (Yes).
Form
Reveal
Phrasal Verb (Separable)
You can say: "Try on the shoes" OR "Try the shoes on". Pronouns must go in the middle: "Try them on".
Task 4: Word 2

The Distinction — fit vs. suit

Meaning
  • If shoes are a Size 10 and you are a Size 10, they fit you. (Physical size/shape).
  • If a yellow shirt makes your eyes look great, it suits you. (Style/Appearance).
Practice
Reveal
"I love the color, it really me, but unfortunately, a size medium doesn't me; it's too tight."
Task 5: Editorial

Behind the Scenes: Fast Sportswear

Read the editorial below. Hover over the dotted text to see the Ukrainian translations!

Sports fashion and sneaker culture have become a massive business. Brands create hype by releasing limited-edition collections.

But the problem with fast fashion is the waste. People buy trendy items, wear them a few times, and throw them away. Synthetic materials do not break down easily in the environment.

Next time you try on a pair of shoes, ask yourself: "Are they just trendy, or do I actually need them?"

Task 6: System Rules

Order of Adjectives

When we use more than one adjective before a noun, we must put them in a specific order. A helpful trick is OSASCOMP.

The OSASCOMP Rule

AcronymCategoryExample
OOpinioncool, ugly, beautiful
SSizebig, small, oversized
AAgenew, old, vintage
SShaperound, square, long
CColorblack, neon, green
OOriginItalian, American
MMaterialleather, cotton, synthetic
PPurposerunning, basketball, sleeping
✅ Correct: "A cool new black leather basketball shoe."
❌ Incorrect: "A leather new cool black shoe."
Note: Native speakers rarely use more than 3 adjectives at once!
Task 7: Syntax Wiring

Order the Gear

Apply your knowledge of OSASCOMP. Put the adjectives in brackets in the correct order.

  • He bought a (black / running / expensive) jacket.
    → He bought an jacket.
  • She was wearing (cotton / comfortable / old) sweatpants.
    → She was wearing sweatpants.
  • I need to buy some (white / tennis / new) shoes.
    → I need to buy some shoes.
Task 8: System Rules

So vs. Such (Review)

Let's quickly review how to add extreme emphasis, as seen in previous lessons.

StructureRuleExample
So + Adjective No noun at the end of the phrase. "These sneakers are so expensive."
Such (a/an) + Adj + Noun Must end with a noun. "They are such expensive sneakers."
