Task 3: Word 1

The Urge — tenesmus

Meaning: A continual or recurrent inclination to evacuate the bowels, caused by disorder of the rectum or other illness.

Pronunciation: /təˈnez.məs/

Context: "The tumor in the lower rectum is causing severe tenesmus."

Task 4: Word 2

The Bleeding — hematochezia

Meaning: The passage of fresh, typically red blood per anus, often mixed with stool.

Pronunciation: /ˌhiː.mə.təˈkiː.zi.ə/

Context: "He presented to the ER with painless hematochezia."

Task 5: Word 3

The Blockage — obstruction

Meaning: A condition where the normal flow of intestinal contents is interrupted.

Collocations: bowel ~, intestinal ~, partial ~.

Task 6: Word 4

The Tumor — malignancy

Meaning: The presence of a malignant (cancerous) tumor; the state of being malignant.

Context: "The biopsy confirmed a high-grade malignancy in the sigmoid colon."

Task 7: Clinical Reading

Case Study: Colorectal Mass

During the colonoscopy, a large, ulcerated mass was visualized in the descending colon, causing a near-total obstruction. The patient's primary symptoms over the past few months included severe fatigue, tenesmus, and recurrent episodes of hematochezia.

We must look into the extent of the disease by ordering a contrast CT of the abdomen and pelvis to rule out hepatic metastases. Based on the macroscopic appearance, the likelihood of malignancy is exceptionally high. If his pain flares up, he is instructed to return to the surgical ward immediately.

Task 8: Grammar Theory 1

Phrasal Verbs: Investigation

Phrasal VerbClinical MeaningExample
Rule outTo exclude a diagnosis.We did a CT scan to rule out cancer.
Look intoTo investigate a symptom/issue.We need to look into why his hemoglobin is dropping.
Follow up (on)To check on a patient's progress.I will follow up on the biopsy results tomorrow.

Task 11: Clinical Communication

Softening the Blow (Hedging)

When delivering difficult oncology news, direct language ("You have cancer") is often softened using introductory phrases.

Transform these blunt statements:

1. "The tumor is malignant." ➔ "Based on the results, it appears that..."

2. "You need surgery immediately." ➔ "Our strongest recommendation right now is to..."

3. "The bowel is obstructed." ➔ "The imaging suggests there might be..."
