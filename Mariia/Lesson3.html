Task 04

The Past Simple

The Past Simple is the engine of storytelling and medical charting. It is used exclusively for actions that are 100% finished in the past.

When to use it:

  • 1. Finished Time Words: Yesterday, last week, in 2022, three hours ago.
  • 2. Narrative Sequences: First I did this, then I did that. (e.g. He fell, hit his head, and lost consciousness).
  • 3. Past Habits/States: I lived in Ukraine for 20 years. I didn't like coffee when I was a student.
FormStructureExample
Affirmative Subject + V2 (-ed or irregular) She took the patient's blood pressure.
Negative Subject + did not (didn't) + V1 The patient didn't sleep well.
Question Did + Subject + V1 ? Did the consultant arrive yet?

Task 06

Pronunciation Lab

Regular verbs end in -ed, but there are three completely different ways to pronounce it. Mastering this is the key to sounding fluent.

Sound 1: /t/

After unvoiced sounds (p, k, f, s, ch, sh, th). It sounds sharp and breathy.

  • check → checked (/t/)
  • ask → asked (/t/)
  • slip → slipped (/t/)

Sound 2: /d/

After voiced sounds (b, g, v, z, m, n, l, r, and vowels). It sounds soft.

  • view → viewed (/d/)
  • play → played (/d/)
  • clean → cleaned (/d/)

Sound 3: /ɪd/ (Extra Syllable!)

CRITICAL: Only use this extra syllable if the base verb already ends in a T or D sound.

  • admit → admitted (ad-mit-id)
  • rent → rented (ren-tid)
  • need → needed (nee-did)

Task 12

The Triage Desk

You are the intake nurse. Your teacher is a patient who just arrived at A&E. Take their history using Past Simple questions.

  • Ask what happened to cause their injury (When did it happen? How did you fall?).
  • Ask about their actions immediately after the injury (Did you hit your head? Did you take any painkillers?).
  • Ask about their past medical history (Have you ever broken a bone before? -> *Wait, that's Present Perfect!* Let's stick to Past Simple: Did you break any bones when you were a child?).
