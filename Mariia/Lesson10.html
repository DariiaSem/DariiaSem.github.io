Task 13 · Concept Check

True or False?

Decide if the statements are True or False.

1. Distal means closer to the trunk.
False — distal = further from the trunk; proximal = closer.
2. A transverse plane is sometimes called an axial plane in reports.
True — it divides the body into superior and inferior parts.
3. The thoracic cavity is the inferior subdivision of the ventral cavity.
False — it is the superior one; the abdominopelvic cavity is inferior.
4. In the supine position the patient lies face up.
True — prone is face down.
5. Childbirth is an example of negative feedback.
False — the body promotes the contractions: positive feedback.
6. Negative feedback is the most common type of feedback in the body.
True — positive feedback loops are much more limited.
7. The smallest working unit of the body is the tissue.
False — it is the cell.

The X-ray was taken in the frontal plane, and a second image was taken in the sagittal plane. The scan shows no injury in the thoracic cavity: the heart and lungs look normal. Right now he is lying in the supine position, and we raise the head of the bed to Fowler's position when he eats. We placed a pillow lateral to the injured arm for support. Please do not let him abduct the arm or flex the elbow. We are checking his vital signs every hour: his temperature is slightly raised, but his blood pressure is stable. His pain was acute in the first hours; if it does not improve, it may become chronic.

2. Which planes were used for the X-ray images?
3. What position is the patient in now?
4. Where is the pillow placed?

Build the Feedback Loop

Label each step of this negative feedback loop (body temperature rises).

Internal body temperature rises above normal.
↓
Thermoreceptors (sensors) in the tissues detect the change.
↓
The thermoregulation centre in the brain is activated and compares the temperature with the set point.
↓
Sweat glands secrete sweat that evaporates, and blood vessels in the skin dilate so heat escapes.
↓
Temperature falls back to about 36–38 °C: homeostasis is restored.

Task 18 · Role-Play

The Radiology Request

You are the ward doctor. Your teacher is the radiographer. Describe the patient, the position and the images you need.

You: Say where the injury is (proximal / distal, which bone) and what position the patient is in.
↓
Radiographer: “Which views do you need?”
↓
You: Ask for images in the frontal and sagittal planes.
↓
Radiographer: “Can he move the arm? How should I position him?”
↓
You: Give instructions: supine; do not abduct the arm or flex the elbow.
↓
Radiographer: “Any other problems? Anything I should monitor?”
↓
You: Summarise using vital signs, acute / chronic pain, and one feedback-loop term.
