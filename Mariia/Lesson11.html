Task 02

The Structural Map

The human body is divided into specific spaces called cavities. Review the primary divisions below.

Primary Body Cavities

  • Cranial Cavity: Located in the head. It protects the brain.
  • Thoracic Cavity: The chest area. It contains the heart and lungs, protected by the rib cage.
  • Abdominal Cavity: Located below the chest. It contains the stomach, liver, intestines, and kidneys.
  • Pelvic Cavity: The lowest section of the torso. It contains the bladder and reproductive organs.

Task 05

Anatomical Directions

Medical professionals do not say above or below. We use precise directional terminology based on the standard anatomical position.

TermOppositeMeaning / Example
Superior Inferior Above / Below.
The heart is superior to the stomach. The bladder is inferior to the kidneys.
Anterior Posterior Front / Back.
The sternum is anterior to the heart. The kidneys are posterior to the intestines.
Medial Lateral Towards the middle / Towards the side.
The heart is medial to the lungs. The lungs are lateral to the heart.
Proximal Distal Closer to the trunk / Further from the trunk (usually for limbs).
The knee is proximal to the ankle. The hand is distal to the elbow.

Task 08

Anatomical Relations

Evaluate these statements regarding the position of organs in the body.

1. The heart is medial to the lungs.
True — the heart is in the center (medial), between the lungs.
2. The liver is superior to the lungs.
False — the liver is in the abdomen, inferior to the lungs.
3. The kidneys are located on the anterior side of the body.
False — they are located on the posterior (back) wall of the abdomen.
4. The bladder is inferior to the stomach.
True — the bladder is in the pelvic cavity, below the abdominal cavity.

Task 10

Reading: The Surgical Handoff

Read this clinical handover note from a trauma surgeon. Pay attention to how anatomical directions are used to pinpoint injuries.

Patient presents with blunt force trauma to the anterior thoracic cavity. Imaging reveals bruising on the medial aspect of the right lung, but the cardiac tissue appears undamaged. The patient also complains of severe pain inferior to the ribcage on the right side. We suspect hepatic injury due to the location. The posterior abdominal wall and renal areas are clear. We will monitor the gastric region for any internal bleeding.

Locating the Pain

Your instructor will play the role of a patient describing their pain using basic, non-medical language. You must translate their complaints into formal anatomical notes.

Patient: It hurts right here in the middle of my chest, behind the bone.
↓
You: Note: Patient reports pain in the medial anterior thoracic cavity.
↓
Patient: I also have a sharp pain in my lower back, on both sides.
↓
You: Note: Patient reports pain in the posterior abdominal wall, likely renal.
