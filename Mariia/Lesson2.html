Task 6: Grammar Structure

Present Simple vs Present Continuous

Clear communication requires accurate tenses. Let us look at the Cambridge rules for when to use these two forms.

TenseCore FunctionMedical / Nursing Example
Present Simple Facts, routines, schedules, permanent situations. The night shift starts at eight in the evening. She works in the pediatric ward.
Present Continuous Actions happening right now, temporary situations, changing states. The patient is responding well to the medication. I am studying nursing in London this year.
⚠️ State Verbs: Remember that verbs describing states, thoughts, or senses usually do not take the continuous form. Examples include know, believe, understand, seem, need. We say: She needs assistance NOT She is needing assistance.

Task 10: Grammar Structure

Present Perfect vs Perfect Continuous

These tenses connect the past to the present. The difference lies in what we want to emphasize: the result or the duration.

TenseCore FunctionMedical / Nursing Example
Present Perfect Focuses on the result of an action, completed actions with relevance to now, or life experiences. Uses have/has + past participle. The doctor has signed the discharge papers. (Result: The papers are signed now). I have worked here for five years.
Present Perfect Continuous Focuses on the duration of an ongoing action, or an action that just stopped with visible present results. Uses have/has been + verb-ing. The patient has been coughing all night. (Focus on duration). I have been studying anatomy for three hours.
⚠️ Key words: We often use for and since with both tenses. We use the Continuous form to emphasize how long something has been happening, especially with dynamic actions like wait, learn, or sleep.

Task 13: Medical Pair

Diagnosis & Chronic

Diagnosis (noun): The identification of the nature of an illness by examination of the symptoms.
Chronic (adjective): Persisting for a long time or constantly recurring (describing an illness).

Read the case files. Select the one that describes a chronic diagnosis.

Case A

The patient presents with asthma, a condition he has managed daily with an inhaler for the past twelve years.

Correct. This condition is long-lasting and requires ongoing care.

Case B

The patient arrived with a severely sprained ankle after a sudden fall on the tennis court.

Incorrect. This is an acute injury, sudden and short-term.
