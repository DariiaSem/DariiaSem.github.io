Task 04

Trauma Sequences

The Past Simple is the absolute core of medical documentation. We use it to record the exact chronological sequence of a trauma response.

Chronological Logging

When multiple actions happen sequentially in the past, they must all be written in the Past Simple. Do not mix tenses.

Event 1: The patient collapsed.

Event 2: The nurse administered oxygen.

Event 3: The doctor stabilized the vitals.

Flow: The patient collapsed, the nurse administered oxygen, and the doctor stabilized the vitals.

Task 06

Pronunciation Lab

Mispronouncing the past tense suffix can cause confusion during critical handoffs. Remember the rules.

Sound 1: /t/

After unvoiced sounds. Sharp and fast.

  • check becomes checked (/t/)
  • collapse becomes collapsed (/t/)

Sound 2: /d/

After voiced sounds. Soft and blended.

  • hemorrhage becomes hemorrhaged (/d/)
  • stabilize becomes stabilized (/d/)

Sound 3: /ɪd/

Creates a completely new syllable. Only use this if the base verb ends in a T or D sound.

  • resuscitate becomes resuscitated (id)
  • administer becomes administered... wait! Administer ends in an R sound. It is an exception? No, administered ends in a /d/ sound. It does not get an extra syllable. Say it: ad-min-is-terd.
Task 07

Syntax Drill 1: Negations

To report what did not happen during an emergency, use did not plus the base verb. Transform these statements into negatives.

  • 1. The patient broke his arm. -> The patient his arm.
  • 2. We administered oxygen. -> We oxygen.
  • 3. She lost consciousness. -> She consciousness.

Syntax Drill 2: Questions

Task 09

The Incident Report

Read the formal incident report. Identify the sequence of events.

The patient arrived at the emergency bay at twenty hours. He sustained severe trauma to the chest and hemorrhaged profusely. The attending team immediately administered clotting agents and stabilized his blood pressure. Shortly after, the patient collapsed and lost consciousness. The team resuscitated him successfully within two minutes. He did not suffer any brain damage and went directly to surgery.

Discussion:

What was the very first action the team took? What happened exactly before they resuscitated him?

Task 11

Speaking: The Handoff

You are transferring your patient to the ICU team. Deliver a rapid summary of events.

  • State exactly what time the patient arrived and what happened to them.
  • List the three actions you took to save them in chronological order.
  • Explain what they did not do (e.g., they did not wake up, they did not speak).
Task 12

Speaking: The Family Update

Your teacher will play the role of a terrified family member. Answer their questions clearly.

  • Family: How did he break his ribs? What did the doctors do when he arrived? Did he suffer?
  • Your goal: Maintain a calm, professional tone. Use Past Simple exclusively to recount the events without causing panic.
