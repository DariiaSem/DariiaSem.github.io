Task 4: Reading

The Year Everything Shifted

Read Marta's short story. As you read, underline every verb and notice which tense it's in — and why.

By the time I turned thirty, I had already changed jobs twice, but neither move had felt like a real watershed moment. I was working as a translator, quietly bored, when an old university friend called out of nowhere. We talked for two hours. Somewhere in that conversation, I had an epiphany: I wanted to retrain as a nurse. It sounds sudden, written like that, but in retrospect, I had been feeling restless for months — I just hadn't named it yet. Since that phone call, I have finished my training, moved cities, and started again from nothing. I still think about that friend sometimes. Funny how a single, ordinary Tuesday can turn out to be so formative.

Task 6: Grammar Theory

Tense Alignment: Stacking Time

A good story doesn't use one tense — it lines several up correctly, relative to each other. Follow the timeline from earliest to now.

Past Perfect
...had already changed jobs...
Past Continuous
...was working as a translator...
Past Simple
...a friend called out of nowhere...
Present Perfect
...I have finished my training...
Earlier past
Now
💡 The logic: Past Perfect = the "before-the-before". Past Continuous = the ongoing background scene. Past Simple = the main event that interrupts or moves the story forward. Present Perfect = the bridge that connects that past to right now.
TenseJob in the StoryExample
Past Perfect Something finished before another past moment She had been in labour for six hours before she arrived.
Past Continuous Background action in progress at a past moment The midwife was examining another patient when she was admitted.
Past Simple The main, completed events of the story Her waters broke. She gave birth six hours later.
Present Perfect Links that past to the present result She has recovered remarkably well since then.
Task 7: Grammar Practice

Error Spy: Tense Alignment

Click the highlighted phrase you think is wrong. Type your correction in the box that appears, then check the explanation.

By the time I arrived at the ward, the shift already finished.

✅ Correction: had already finished

💡 The finishing happened before another past moment (arriving), so we need Past Perfect.

She was living in Kraków for three years before she moved to Warsaw.

✅ Correction: had been living / had lived

💡 A duration completed before another past event needs Past Perfect (Continuous).

The patient was pacing the corridor when the contractions were intensifying suddenly.

✅ Correction: intensified

💡 A sudden, single completed action interrupting the background scene needs Past Simple, not Past Continuous.

Since her diagnosis, she changed her whole outlook on life.

✅ Correction: has changed

💡 "Since" + an unfinished time period pointing to now needs Present Perfect.

I am realising only now, in retrospect, how formative that year had been.

✅ Correction: realise

💡 "Realise" is a stative verb of thinking/mental state — it doesn't usually take the continuous form.

Task 8: Case File Reading

Field Notes: Room 4, 3 a.m.

A resident's private notes on a night shift on the labour ward. Read and notice how the tenses stack to keep the timeline honest.

By the time Mrs. Kowalska reached us, she had been experiencing mild contractions for almost two days at home — she hadn't wanted to "make a fuss" too early, she told me, smiling. When she arrived, the midwife on duty was already examining another woman in the next room, so I took the initial history myself. Her waters broke shortly after admission, and things unfolded quickly from there. Six hours later, she gave birth to a healthy baby girl. It has been one of the more poignant nights of my residency so far — I still think about the calm way she waited, in retrospect, for exactly the right moment to come in.

True, False, or Not Given?

Task 12: Pronunciation

Weak Forms in Narrative Tenses

Based on Avery & Ehrlich's principles of connected speech: in fast, natural storytelling, auxiliary verbs almost disappear. Practise these reductions aloud.

Full FormNatural ReductionIn a Sentence
I had already left I'd already left /aɪd/ I'd already left when she called.
She would have stayed She would've stayed /ˈwʊdəv/ She would've stayed longer, in retrospect.
They have been waiting They've been waiting /ðeɪv/ They've been waiting since six this morning.
🗣️ Word stress: "in RETrospect" stresses the first syllable of retrospect, not the verb-like second syllable. Compare: reTROspect (incorrect) vs. RETrospect (correct).
