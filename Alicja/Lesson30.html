Tasks 5 & 6 · Reading & Comp

The Missing Scrub Cap

Read this anecdote from a senior surgeon. Notice how past speech is reported.

Dr. Miller was a brilliant surgeon, but he had one strange quirk: he refused to operate without his faded blue cap. He told me that he had never lost a patient while wearing it. I tried to rationalize it as a psychological placebo.

One Friday, facing a full moon, he panicked. He exclaimed that someone had stolen his cap. I assured him that he was the best surgeon here and that a hat didn't dictate fate. "Don't jinx it," he muttered, knocking on wood. He warned me that praising a good streak usually caused it to end.

The surgery went perfectly. Afterward, I asked him if he realized his fear had just been a self-fulfilling prophecy that thankfully failed. He pulled the cap out of his own pocket where he had absentmindedly stuffed it, and said, "Confirmation bias is a hell of a drug, isn't it?"

True or False?

1. Dr. Miller believed praising a perfect record brought good luck.
False! He said praising it would "jinx" it (cause it to end).
2. The narrator actually believed the cap had magical powers.
False! The narrator tried to rationalize it as a "placebo".
Task 7 · Grammar

Tense Alignment (Backshifting)

When recounting stories, gossip, or medical histories, we shift tenses backward one step in time to reflect that the speech happened in the past.

Original (Direct Quote)Reported History (Backshifted)
"I have never lost a patient."
Present Perfect		He told me he had never lost a patient.
Past Perfect
"Someone stole my cap!"
Past Simple		He exclaimed that someone had stolen his cap.
Past Perfect
"I am stuffing it in my pocket."
Present Continuous		He admitted he was stuffing it in his pocket.
Past Continuous
🚀 The "Universal Truth" Exception: If the fact is still biologically/universally true *now*, you don't backshift.
Example: "The doctor explained that full moons do not cause higher birth rates."
