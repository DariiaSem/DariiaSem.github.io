01. THE MINDSET

The Paradox of Choice

Analyze the psychology of modern brand marketing. Discuss the dilemma with your partner.

Look at the evolution of corporate logos (Apple, Google, Starbucks). They have transitioned from detailed, 3D designs to flat, simplistic shapes. Consumers complain that brands have lost their "soul" and look identical.

Discussion: Why do marketing teams strip away details? Is minimalism a genuine choice to reduce cognitive load, or just a lazy, cost-saving trend?

03. COMPREHENSION

Fact vs. Fiction

1. The CMO redesigned the interface herself.
FALSE. "We had the agency redesign the interface." She arranged for someone else to do it.
2. The design team initially believed minimalism looked incomplete.
TRUE. "The team resisted... arguing it looked unfinished."

06. SYSTEM RULES

Passive Causative

Used when we arrange for a service, or experience an event out of our control. Focus is on the object.

Have/Get + Object + V3

We had the website redesigned.
Arranging a service (paid/asked)"The team got the campaign launched."
Experiencing a negative event"We had our servers hacked."
07. SYSTEM RULES

Active Causative

Focus is on WHO we assigned to do the task. Have vs. Get changes the meaning.

HAVE + Person + V1 (Base)GET + Person + TO + V1
Authority/Instruction: It's their job. No persuasion.

"I had the intern print reports."		Persuasion: You had to convince or trick them.

"I got the client to agree."
⚠️ Golden Rule: Get takes to. Have never takes to.
08. SYSTEM RULES

Make, Let, Force, Allow

Make vs. Force

Make + Person + V1: Authority/Require. "They made us sign."

Force + Person + TO + V1: Pressure/Threats. "They forced us to delete it."

Let vs. Allow

Let + Person + V1: Permission (Informal). "They let me leave."

Allow + Person + TO + V1: Permission (Formal). "Policy allows staff to work."

Elevate the Language

Rewrite using C1 causatives.

  1. B2: Someone designed a new logo for us last week.
    C1 [Passive Causative]: We
  2. B2: I persuaded the influencer to promote our brand.
    C1 [Active Causative - GET]: I
  3. B2: The manager told the copywriter to delete the text.
    C1 [Active Causative - HAVE]: The manager
12. PHONETICS

The Flow of Fluency

Native speakers heavily reduce causatives.

"Got them to" → /gɒtəm tə/

"We finally /gɒtəm tə/ sign the contract."

"Had him" → /hædɪm/

"I /hædɪm/ remove the graphics." (Drop 'H').

13. SIMULATION

The Agency Showdown

You are negotiating a campaign aesthetic. Deadline is tomorrow.

The Pragmatic Marketer

You believe the campaign is cluttered and ostentatious. You want an ascetic design. Use Active Causatives to tell the Director what you'll make the team do. ("I'll have the copywriters delete this...")

The Art Director

You think minimalism is boring. Use Passive Causatives to defend the money you spent. ("We had the logo custom-designed! We got the photography curated!")
