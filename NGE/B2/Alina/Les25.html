Before discussing money, let's fix these clinical sentences using the Advanced Inversion structures from Lesson 24.

  • Type 1 (Polite Future): If you should need extra composite, let me know.

The Certification Trap

QUERY: How do you break this cycle? Do you take on a loan, work extra weekend shifts, or ask the clinic owner to sponsor you?

Lifestyle Creep

QUERY: Have you observed this "lifestyle creep" among your peers? Is it a psychological necessity to reward yourself after working hard?

Lucrative
/ˈluː.krə.tɪv/
Producing a great deal of profit; highly financially rewarding.
Concept Checking
  • If an investment pays you $5 a year, is it lucrative? (No).
  • Is assisting in high-end aesthetics generally considered a lucrative niche?
Collocations: a highly ~ business, a ~ contract, prove ~.
Squander
/ˈskwɒn.dər/
To waste (money, time, or an opportunity) in a reckless and foolish manner.
Concept Checking
  • If you buy shares in a secure index fund, are you squandering your money?
  • If you spend your entire bonus on a luxury trip while in debt, did you squander it?
Collocations: to ~ a fortune, to ~ an opportunity, ~ savings.
Volatility
/ˌvɒl.əˈtɪl.ə.ti/
Liability to change rapidly and unpredictably, especially for the worse (in markets or emotions).
Concept Checking
  • If a stock's price stays at exactly $10 for a year, does it have high volatility?
  • Why does high market volatility make conservative investors nervous?
Collocations: market ~, high ~, price ~. Adj: Volatile.
Liquidate
/ˈlɪk.wɪ.deɪt/
To convert assets (like stocks or property) into cash, often to pay off debts or make a large purchase.
Concept Checking
  • If you own an apartment, can you buy groceries with it directly? (No).
  • You must liquidate the asset first. What do you get in return? (Cash).
Collocations: to ~ assets, to ~ a company, forced liquidation.

Task 08

Unreal Time: Wish & It's Time

We use specific verb patterns to talk about regrets, desires, and urgent complaints. In these structures, the tense steps backwards to show that the situation is unreal or hypothetical.

1. Wish / If Only

Present Dissatisfaction

Wish / If only + Past Simple

"I wish I earned a higher base salary right now."

(Reality: I do not earn a high salary)
Past Regret

Wish / If only + Past Perfect

"If only I hadn't squandered my bonus last year."

(Reality: I did squander it)

2. It's (high / about) time

Used to say that something should be done urgently, or should have been done already. Even though it refers to the present/future, we use the Past Simple.

Formula: It's (high) time + Subject + Past Simple

"It's high time you started investing your money."

(Wrong: It's high time you start)

Task 09

The Subjunctive Mood

In formal, academic, or professional English, when we use verbs of command or adjectives of urgency, the following verb drops all conjugation (no 's', no '-ed', no 'ing'). It remains in its raw, base form.

Normal: The doctor insists that she liquidates the asset.
↓
Subjunctive: The doctor insists that she liquidate the asset.
Normal: It is crucial that he is careful.
↓
Subjunctive: It is crucial that he be careful.

Trigger Words

The subjunctive is triggered by a specific set of words followed by "that":

  • Verbs: demand, insist, propose, recommend, suggest, request.
  • Adjectives: crucial, essential, imperative, important, vital.
  • Negatives: Simply put "not" before the base verb (e.g., "I suggest she not sell the stocks").

Subjunctive Drill

Apply the formal subjunctive mood. Pay close attention to negative and 'be' verbs.

  • Subjunctive (Standard): The manager demands that every assistant (arrive) 15 minutes early.
  • Subjunctive (Negative): The financial advisor strongly recommended that she (not squander) her bonus on luxury goods.

Phonetic Firewall

These financial and professional terms are notoriously difficult for Ukrainian speakers due to silent letters and shifting stress. Practice them out loud with your instructor.

Debt
/det/
Trap: The 'b' is completely 100% silent. Do not say "debt". It sounds exactly like "det".
Receipt
/rɪˈsiːt/

Lucrative
/ˈluː.krə.tɪv/
Trap: The stress is heavily on the FIRST syllable. LU-cra-tive. Not lu-CRA-tive.
Squander
/ˈskwɒn.dər/
