Mixed Conditionals Theory & Practice

Why can't we just use the 2nd or 3rd Conditional?

Standard conditionals assume the condition and the result happen in the same timeframe.

  • 2nd Conditional: Present/Future Condition ➔ Present/Future Result. (If I had a car, I would drive.)
  • 3rd Conditional: Past Condition ➔ Past Result. (If I had studied, I would have passed.)

But life is messy! Sometimes a past decision affects the present, or a general fact alters a past event. When the timelines don't match, we are forced to mix them.

Type If-Clause (Condition) Main Clause (Result) Usage
Type 3 + 2 Past Perfect
If + had + V3		 Present Conditional
would + V1		 A past action has a result in the present.
Type 2 + 3 Past Simple
If + V2 / weren't		 Perfect Conditional
would have + V3		 A general present fact changed something in the past.
