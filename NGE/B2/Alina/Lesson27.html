Reactive vs. Preventive: Is the current medical system built to keep people healthy, or just to fix them when they break?

Task 4 · Reading

The Case of Mrs. Kovalenko

Read this clinical narrative. Pay close attention to how the tenses shift to establish a precise timeline of the patient's deterioration before she arrived at the clinic.

By the time Mrs. Kovalenko, a sprightly 92-year-old, arrived at our clinic on Tuesday, she had been experiencing severe discomfort under her lower denture for three weeks. She told us that she had noticed a slight looseness initially, but she had ignored it, assuming it was a normal part of senescence.


While Dr. Aris was examining her oral mucosa, he noted that the alveolar bone resorption had progressed significantly since her last visit two years prior. We were looking at a classic case of neglected geriatric maintenance. For years, she had been relying on over-the-counter adhesives rather than seeking prophylactic care.


Because her susceptibility to infection had increased due to a recent bout of pneumonia, immediate intervention was required. We decided to initiate a soft reline procedure to prevent further degenerative tissue damage before complete edentulism complications made a new prosthesis impossible.

Task 5 · Verification

Clinical Chart Review

Verify the timeline of events based on the clinical narrative.

1. Mrs. Kovalenko came to the clinic immediately after noticing the loose denture.
False. She had been experiencing discomfort for three weeks and had initially ignored it.
2. The bone loss (resorption) happened while the doctor was examining her.
False. The resorption "had progressed" over the previous two years.
3. She had been using pharmacy adhesives instead of getting professional adjustments.
True. "She had been relying on over-the-counter adhesives..."
4. Her recent pneumonia made her more vulnerable to oral infections.
True. Her "susceptibility to infection had increased due to a recent bout of pneumonia."

Task 7 · System Rules

Narrative Tenses Matrix

To write accurate medical histories or tell a compelling story, you must interlock these four tenses to map out exactly *when* things happened relative to each other.

TenseStructureClinical Use Case
Past Simple V2 / -ed The anchor point. The specific event that happened.
"The patient arrived at 9 AM."
Past Continuous was/were + V-ing The background. What was happening when the main event occurred.
"While I was preparing the tray, the patient arrived."
Past Perfect had + V3 The 'Before' Past. An event completed *before* the anchor point.
"He arrived at 9 AM. He had forgotten his X-rays."
Past Perfect Cont. had been + V-ing The build-up. A continuous action leading up to the anchor point.
"He had been ignoring the pain for months before he arrived."
⚠️ The Golden Rule: You almost never use the Past Perfect on its own. It needs a Past Simple "anchor point" to reference. Without a Past Simple event, you are just floating in time!
