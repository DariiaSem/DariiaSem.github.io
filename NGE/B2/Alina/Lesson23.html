Task 8: Editorial

The Gateway to the Gut

Read the clinical editorial below. Pay close attention to how the author uses Discourse Markers (highlighted in gold) to connect complex ideas, and notice the advanced medical vocabulary.

The Periodontal Review // Holistic Approaches

For generations, dentistry operated as an isolated discipline. Nevertheless, modern research has unequivocally demonstrated that the oral cavity is merely the beginning of the gastrointestinal tract. The relationship between oral microbiomes and gut health is highly bidirectional. If a patient presents with chronic periodontitis, swallowing pathogenic bacteria daily disrupts the gut flora. Consequently, this dysbiosis triggers a systemic inflammatory response affecting the entire body.

I was speaking with a senior periodontist last week, and he mentioned that the clinic was going to mandate nutritional counseling for all severe cases. He argued that treating the gums without addressing the diet was futile. Be that as it may, many dental assistants feel unequipped to give dietary advice. Conversely, progressive clinics are now training staff to suggest anti-inflammatory basics—such as replacing processed meats with high-quality proteins like lentils, eggs, feta, and soft-salted salmon.

Creating an environment conducive to healing requires us to look beyond the teeth. We must ask about stress, sleep, and digestion to understand the true etiology of oral diseases. Ultimately, treating the mouth means treating the whole patient.

Discussion:

1. Look at the phrase "he mentioned that the clinic was going to mandate...". Why didn't the author write "is going to"?

2. Which discourse marker above is used to show a result? Which ones are used to show contrast or concession?

Task 9: System Rules

Discourse Markers (C1 Level)

At the C1 level, simple connectors like "but" or "so" are insufficient for academic or clinical writing. We use advanced discourse markers to signal precise logical relationships.

1. Concession (Accepting a point, but introducing a counter-point)

MarkerUsage & MeaningExample
Nevertheless / Nonetheless Despite what has just been said. (Very formal). The procedure carries risks. Nevertheless, it is necessary to stop the systemic infection.
Be that as it may Acknowledges a fact, but dismisses it as a reason to change course. ("Even if that is true...") The patient dislikes flossing. Be that as it may, we must insist upon it.

2. Contrast & Result

MarkerUsage & MeaningExample
Conversely Introduces a statement that is the exact opposite of the previous one. Poor gut health harms the gums. Conversely, a healthy diet protects them.
Consequently / Thus Shows the direct result or effect. Bacteria enter the bloodstream. Consequently, inflammation spreads.
Task 10: System Rules

Future in the Past & Reported Speech

When reporting conversations from the past about plans that were supposed to happen in the future, we must shift our tenses backwards. This is called the Future in the Past.

"will"
→
would
"am/is/are going to"
→
was/were going to
"am/is/are + V-ing"
→
was/were + V-ing
Direct Speech (At the time)Reported Speech (Now)
The dentist said, "I will prescribe probiotics." He said that he would prescribe probiotics.
The manager told me, "We are going to change the protocol." She informed me that they were going to change the protocol.
The patient said, "I am seeing a nutritionist tomorrow." He mentioned that he was seeing a nutritionist the following day.
⚠️ Advanced Note: If the plan from the past still hasn't happened yet and is still relevant today, you don't always have to shift the tense backwards. (e.g., "Yesterday he told me he is coming in next week.") However, shifting to "was" is always grammatically safe in formal C1 reporting.

Task 12: Simulation

The Holistic Consultation

Work with your partner to navigate this clinical scenario. You must address the patient's systemic health while staying within your professional boundaries.

The Dental Assistant

You are taking the patient's medical history. They have severe, recurring gum inflammation, but their oral hygiene is perfect. You suspect a systemic issue related to their high-stress lifestyle and poor diet. You want to suggest an environment conducive to healing without diagnosing them with a medical disease.

Grammar: Use Hedging ("I tend to think", "It appears that") and Discourse Markers ("Be that as it may", "Consequently").

The Exhausted Patient

You are frustrated. You brush and floss perfectly. You work 70 hours a week and survive on fast food and coffee. Three weeks ago, a different dentist told you that your gums were going to heal, but they haven't. You don't understand the bidirectional link between your stomach issues and your mouth.

Grammar: Report past promises using Future in the Past ("He told me it would...").
