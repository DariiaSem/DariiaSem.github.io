Task 04

Passive Voice Matrix (Full Audit)

The passive voice shifts focus from *who* did the action to *what* received the action. The core formula is always: Form of BE + Past Participle (V3).

TenseActive ExamplePassive StructurePassive Example
Present Simple They update the software. am / is / are + V3 The software is updated.
Past Simple They updated the software. was / were + V3 The software was updated.
Continuous (Pres/Past) They are/were updating it. am/is/are/was/were + being + V3 The software is/was being updated.
Perfect (Pres/Past) They have/had updated it. have/has/had + been + V3 The software has/had been updated.
Modals/Future They will/must update it. will/must + be + V3 The software will/must be updated.
Task 05

Concept Check: Passives

Decide if these grammatical statements are True or False.

1. In a passive sentence, the person performing the action (the agent) must always be mentioned using "by".
False — We frequently omit the agent if it is obvious, unknown, or unimportant.
2. "The clinic was being renovated" is an example of the Past Continuous Passive.
True — was/were + being + V3.
3. "The report has written" is a correct Present Perfect Passive sentence.
False — It must be "has BEEN written". Without "been", it is active (the report wrote something).

Task 07

Advanced Passives: Impersonal & Causative

At the C1 level, we frequently use these two specific passive structures to sound highly professional, objective, or to describe delegating tasks.

The Impersonal Passive

Used in formal reports or news to state general beliefs, rumors, or unverified facts without attributing them to a specific person.

  • Structure 1 (It + Passive + That): It is said that the new CEO will arrive tomorrow. (Instead of: People say that...)
  • Structure 2 (Subject + Passive + Infinitive): The new CEO is expected to arrive tomorrow.
  • Common Verbs: say, believe, expect, think, know, report.

The Causative (Have/Get something done)

Used when you arrange for someone else to do a service for you. You don't do it yourself.

  • Formula: Subject + Have/Get (in any tense) + Object + Past Participle (V3).
  • Active meaning: The mechanic repaired my car.
  • Causative meaning: I had my car repaired. (I didn't fix it myself).
  • Clinic context: We are getting the scanners upgraded next week.

Reading: The Innovation Memo

Read this internal clinic memo. Pay attention to how Passives and Reported Speech are used to sound professional and objective.

To: All Clinic Staff
Subject: Workflow Optimization & New Protocols

It has been reported by several senior assistants that our current patient-intake process has stagnated. Last week, I met with the IT consultants. They advised us that our legacy software is creating a severe bottleneck. Consequently, a highly disruptive, cloud-based platform will be installed this weekend. We are having the entire network reconfigured by external specialists.

During the meeting, Dr. Weber expressed concern about the transition. He asked if the clinic would be closed on Monday. I assured him that the downtime had already been accounted for, and that no appointments would need to be canceled. It is expected that this upgrade will serve as a catalyst for significantly streamlining our daily operations.

Task 13 · Role-Play

The Pitch

Act out this scenario. You are an intrapreneur pitching a disruptive new software to your highly conservative boss.

You: Explain that the current workflow is stagnating. Use an Impersonal Passive ("It is widely believed that our system...").
↓
Boss: Push back. Remind them that last time new tech was installed, it was a disaster.
↓
You: Agree, but use Reported Speech to relay what external IT specialists told you about this new system (e.g. "The consultants assured me that it had been tested...").
↓
Boss: Ask who will manage the setup and training.
↓
You: Answer using a Causative Passive ("We will have the network configured by..."). Explain how it will act as a catalyst.
