Task 05: Editorial

The Cost of Transparency

When the data leak occurred, the PR department found itself in a terrible quandary. The analytics firm had legally, but deceptively, acquired millions of user profiles. Management suggested burying the apology on page 40 of a standard update, arguing that drawing attention to it was utterly foolish.

Seemingly, this strategy worked for a week. But soon, independent watchdogs began to scrutinize the code. The repercussions were exceptionally severe. Arguably, the cover-up did more damage than the initial leak. The company's integrity was deeply compromised in the eyes of the public.

It could be argued that complete transparency is highly impractical in modern business. However, in an era of hyper-visibility, lying is bitterly expensive.

Task 06: Comprehension

Fact vs. Fiction

1. The analytics firm acquired the user profiles illegally.
FALSE. They acquired them "legally, but deceptively."
2. The author states that total transparency is always the easiest strategy in business.
FALSE. The text hedges: "It could be argued that complete transparency is highly impractical..."

Extreme Intensifiers

At C1 level, "very" and "really" are insufficient. We use specific adverbs that collocate strongly with certain adjectives.

Task 09: System Rules

The Art of Hedging

Hedging is cautious language. It softens claims, protecting you from being proven wrong in professional environments.

Softening Claims

Diplomacy
Hedging DeviceUsageExample
Arguably / ConceivablySuggests a strong possibility without claiming absolute fact.This is arguably the worst scandal of the decade.
Seemingly / OstensiblyAppears to be true, but implies there might be a hidden reality.Seemingly, the problem is fixed. (But maybe it isn't).
To some extentLimits the scope of a statement.To some extent, the consumers are also to blame.
It could be argued thatIntroduces a controversial opinion neutrally.It could be argued that deleting the data was illegal.

Task 13: Phonetics

Stressing the Intensity

When using extreme intensifiers, native speakers use heavy emotional intonation. The adverb receives the primary sentence stress.

"It is UTTERLY ridiculous."

The word UTTERLY is stressed heavily. The pitch drops at the end of the sentence to signify absolute certainty and disgust.

"We are BITTERLY opposed."

The word BITTERLY is stretched out to show extreme frustration. Practice this sentence with your partner.
