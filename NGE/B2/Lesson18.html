Task 01

Recap Relay: Participles

Before diving into memories, let us refine sentences using the Perfect Participles learned in Lesson 17.

  • Perfect Participle: After she had found the old photograph, she started to cry. (Having found...) the old photograph, she started to cry.
  • Negative Perfect: Because he had not visited his hometown for a decade, he felt like a stranger. (Not having visited...) his hometown for a decade, he felt like a stranger.
  • Present Participle: Because it hopes to preserve the past, the museum archives everything. (Hoping to...) preserve the past, the museum archives everything.
  • Past Participle: Because he was exhausted by the memories, he closed the journal. (Exhausted by...) the memories, he closed the journal.
Task 02

Diagnosing the Past

Humans interact with their histories in profoundly different ways. Discuss these two archetypes with your partner.

Archetype A: The Archivist

They keep every ticket stub, every handwritten note, and every blurry photograph. Their physical space is heavily cluttered with tangible anchors to bygone eras.

Discussion: Does keeping physical objects genuinely preserve memory, or does it prevent a person from moving forward?

Archetype B: The Eraser

They regularly purge their camera roll. They throw away old letters and prefer living strictly in the present moment. They rely solely on internal recall.

Discussion: Is this approach healthy detachment, or does it risk a complete loss of personal history over time?

Task 06

The Attic Archive

Read the narrative below. Notice how the author uses Narrative Tenses (highlighted in indigo) to organize timelines.

The rain was drumming heavily against the roof when I finally found the old wooden box. I had been searching for it all afternoon in my grandmother's attic.

Inside, beneath a stack of letters, lay a highly evocative photograph. I realized immediately that I had seen this picture before, many years ago. It showed two young women laughing in a sunlit park. They had clearly just finished a long journey, as their boots were covered in mud.

As I stared at the indelible image, I began to reminisce. I knew that memory is often rose-tinted, but in that moment, the joy captured on paper felt absolute and real.

Syntax Analysis 1

Look at the phrase was drumming. Why is this in the Past Continuous instead of Past Simple (drummed)? What function does it serve in the story?

Syntax Analysis 2

Look at had been searching. How is this different from had searched? Does it emphasize a quick action or an ongoing duration before another past event?

The Nature of Recall

The author mentions that memory is often idealized. Do you agree that photographs capture the truth, or do they only capture the best moments?

Task 07

Setting the Scene

At the C1 level, weaving narrative tenses seamlessly is essential. The foundation consists of the Past Continuous (for background) and the Past Simple (for main events).

1. Background vs. Action

We use the Past Continuous (was/were + V-ing) to paint the picture, and the Past Simple (V2) for the actions that interrupt or move the story forward.

The wind was howling through the trees when I finally opened the diary.

Task 09

The Pluperfect

To jump backward in a story, we use the Pluperfect (Past Perfect). It clarifies which action happened before the main past time.

Action 1: She locked the door.
Action 2: I arrived.
→
When I arrived, she had locked the door.
Action 1: He was waiting for hours.
Action 2: I found him.
→
When I found him, he had been waiting for hours.

Simple vs. Continuous

  • Past Perfect Simple (had + V3) Focuses on the completion or the result before a past moment. (e.g. I realized I had seen the photo before.)
  • Past Perfect Continuous (had been + V-ing) Focuses on the duration of an activity leading up to a past moment. (e.g. I had been searching for it all afternoon.)

Task 12

The Reunion Standoff

Work with your partner to navigate this nostalgic dispute at a 10-year reunion.

The Realist

You remember high school exactly as it was: stressful, difficult, and full of drama. You think your friend has an entirely rose-tinted view of the past. Correct their memories.

Grammar Target: Use the Past Perfect to establish facts before other events (e.g., By the time we graduated, we had already stopped speaking).

The Nostalgic

You love to reminisce. You believe your youth was a golden era of friendship and fun. Certain songs are highly evocative for you, and the memories are indelible.

Grammar Target: Use the Past Continuous to set beautiful scenes (e.g., We were always laughing while we were studying).
