Task 01

Recap Relay: Cleft Sentences

Before diving into freelance survival, let's emphasize our frustrations using Cleft Sentences (It was / What) from Lesson 16.

  • Focus on the subject: The project manager ruined the deployment. (It was the...) who ruined the deployment.
  • Focus on the action: I need a higher hourly rate. (What I...) is a higher hourly rate.
  • Focus on the reason: I quit because of the endless meetings. (It was because...) that I quit.
  • Focus on the time: We noticed the bug on Friday. (It wasn't until...) that we noticed the bug.

The Developer's Dilemma

Blog // Confessions of a Tech Nomad

Driven by the promise of complete autonomy, I left my corporate job three years ago. I thought I had escaped the system. However, working 80 hours a week just to maintain a basic income, I soon realized I had merely traded one master for many.

The precarity is exhausting. Having deployed five major features for a startup last month, I expected fair remuneration. Instead, they ghosted me. My highly specialized skills are now entirely commoditized by gig platforms.

Task 07

Participle Clauses (Present & Past)

Participle clauses are a hallmark of advanced (C1+) English. They allow us to express condition, reason, or time in a highly concise, elegant way by removing conjunctions (because, while, when).

1. Present Participles (V-ing)

Used for active actions happening at the same time, or to show a cause/reason.

Because he worked 80 hours a week, he burned out.

"Working 80 hours a week, he burned out."

2. Past Participles (V3 / -ed)

Used for passive meanings or to describe states/feelings caused by something else.

Because she was driven by autonomy, she quit her job.

"Driven by autonomy, she quit her job."

Task 09

Perfect Participles

When we want to emphasize that one action was completely finished before the second action started, we use the Perfect Participle.

After I had signed the NDA
→
Having signed the NDA
Because she had failed twice
→
Having failed twice
Since we hadn't received pay
→
Not having received pay

Important Rules

  • Rule 1: Shared Subject The subject of the participle clause must be the same as the main clause. (e.g. Having finished the code, the client paid me. = WRONG. This implies the client finished the code!)
  • Rule 2: Negative Form To make it negative, simply put "Not" before "having". (e.g., Not having saved any money, precarity hit hard.)
